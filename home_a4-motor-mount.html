A4: Motor Mount

For this assignment I was tasked with designing a motor mount for this specific  motor Links to an external site.. I was given a choice between ABS, PETG, and PLA for the material of my motor so I ended up going with PLA Links to an external site. because I knew it was a strong, easily accessible, and I already had some experience with this material so it felt like the obvious option. The assembly of the motor and the mount should look something like this at the end

 

 

 

With the given dimensions of...  

First I decided what the lengths of both features are going to be. The only factor that plays into the lengths is the size of the motor so I saw that the length of the gearbox was 28 mm so I decided to make the bottom part of my mount 30 mm. Using the same logic for feature 2 I saw that the combined height of the motor and the gearbox was about 74.6 mm so I decided to make my second feature 80 mm. Now I had to see what the widths of my features are going to be. For this I had to consider 2 things the length of my shaft and how thick it needs to be to be resistant to the stress from bending that the motor is applying. Knowing this I can start the designing process.

Plugging all of my values in that I stated in my knowns chart I got 

9.14 mm bending stress

17.96 mm from bending deflection which is what I will be using for the width of the first feature 

Designing feature #2

Using the same knowns and unknowns I can solve for the width of feature 2 the same way I solved for feature 1, with the only thing changing being the moment. Using the FBD below I determine the moment at the arbitrary point where the mount will be bolted to the wall

Using the equations for bending due to stress and deflection I get

13.28 mm from stress bending

23.04 mm from deflection bending which is what I will be using for my width 

Using these dimensions I am now able to make an isometric sketch of my motor mount 

 

Parametric modeling

Below I have my equations solved by SolidWorks. I initially converted my values to meters, which gave me very small results—so small that some appeared to be zero when rounded. This was due to the scale of my geometry being much smaller compared to a full meter. Once I converted those same values into millimeters, the numbers made sense and I was able to obtain the correct results in my calculations.

And just like that my motor mount was complete!

 

Lessons learned 

One lesson I learned was about how messy the design process can get. At one point I forgot to save a part file and had to completely restart my CAD model, which was frustrating but also pushed me to rethink some of my choices and improve the design. Going through that showed me that mistakes and do-overs are just part of engineering, and sometimes they actually lead to a cleaner, more thought-out final product.
