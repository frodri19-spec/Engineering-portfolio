Next I had to find the Cross sectional area of the elements using a safety factor of 3.5. To do that I must first list my knowns and unknowns 

Knowns

Unknowns

In order to find the cross sectional area I needed to find the member that was applying the largest amount of force and to do that I needed to find the force that each member was applying on my truss 

With each piece of information that the truss gave me I was able to find the angles that the members were acting on certain joints and was able to calculate exactly how much force each member was receiving by summing the forces in the x and they y direction for each joint 

I could clearly tell that member CA was applying the largest force on my truss so I chose that value to implement into my yield strength equation

Lessons learned 

This project was one of my first true engineering experiences, and it taught me the importance of verifying all given constraints before starting the calculations. At first, I went straight into applying the equations without realizing one of the boundary conditions was inconsistent, and I did not catch the mistake until I was already more than halfway through. Having to restart showed me how critical it is to slow down and carefully review the setup before solving. At the same time, this was the first time I was able to apply some of the formulas I had only seen in class, such as equilibrium equations and stress calculations, to an actual design problem. Putting theory into practice made me realize how much attention to detail matters when moving from the classroom to a real application.

This assignment took me about 13 hours
